One row per study, named however your sheet names it

What sm reads, and what it does when a column is missing

1

Lay it out the way you extracted it

One row per study for a prognostic factor, one row per validation for a model. What each row needs is whatever that paper happened to report, and papers do not agree. One gives the interval, the next the standard error, a third neither and leaves you the counts to work back from.

A prognostic factor: ratios with their limits

# metamisc::Zhang, 20 studies
Study                   N    HR  HR.025  HR.975
Gonzalez-Rodilla 2013  126  1.14    0.52    2.52
Togami 2012             71  3.00    1.15    7.01
Mori 2010               63  2.58    1.03    7.92
Konecny 2009           273  2.13    1.65    2.73
...

No standard error anywhere in the table. easyPR recovers it from the interval, on the log scale, where the interval is symmetric.

A model: whatever each validation reported

# metamisc::EuroSCORE, 23 validations
Study        n  n.events  e.events  c.index  se.c.index
Nashef    5553       232     221.3    0.809      0.0138
Biancari  1027        28      46.0    0.867      0.0351
Di Dedda  1090        41      34.0    0.810      0.0356
...

The same table answers two different questions. With sm = "cstat" easyPR reads the c-statistic columns, with sm = "OE" it reads the counts.

2

You do not rename your columns first

metamisc wants columns called cstat, cstat.se, N, O and E. Almost nobody names them that. Its own EuroSCORE data does not. So easyPR recognises the common spellings, and prints the mapping rather than applying it silently.

What it wantsSpellings it recognises
cstatc.index, cindex, c.statistic, AUC, auc
cstat.sese.c.index, se.cstat, se.auc, seAUC
Nn, n.total, sample.size
On.events, observed, events
Ee.events, expected
the ratioHR, OR, RR, ratio, estimate, TE
its limitsHR.025 and HR.975, ci.lb and ci.ub, lower and upper
> fit <- easypr(EuroSCORE, sm = "cstat") easypr: read c.index as cstat, se.c.index as cstat.se, c.index.95CIl as cstat.cilb, c.index.95CIu as cstat.ciub, n as N, n.events as O, e.events as E.

When your sheet holds both an odds ratio and a hazard ratio, the one you named in sm is the one that is read.

3

When the table cannot answer, it says so

An error is a result.

A sheet of hazard ratios holds no c-statistic. There is nothing to pool, so easyPR names the columns it looked for and stops. A package that guessed here would hand you a number, and the number would mean nothing.
> easypr(ma$zhang, sm = "cstat") Error: easypr: no validation in `data` ended up with both an estimate and a standard error for cstat. Check that the columns hold numbers rather than text.

The same happens to a measure easyPR does not pool, and to a pooling method these measures cannot use. Both refusals name what you asked for and what is available, rather than falling back on a default.

> easypr(ma$zhang, sm = "SMD") Error: easypr: `sm = "SMD"` is not a measure easyPR pools. Use one of: HR, OR, RR, cstat, OE. "HR", "OR" and "RR" pool a prognostic factor; "cstat" and "OE" pool a model's discrimination and its calibration.
A study that reported too little is dropped, counted, and named in the message. It is never quietly left out of the denominator.
